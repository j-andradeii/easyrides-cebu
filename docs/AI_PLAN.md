# Using AI in the EasyRide CRM — a realistic plan

**Scope:** the admin portal at `src/app/admin/**`.
**Written against the system as it actually is**, not as a generic CRM.

---

## TL;DR

Production today holds **3 contacts, 7 leads, 3 quotes, 3 payments, 0 reviews, 0 referrals**.
Almost every "AI for CRM" article assumes thousands of closed deals and a pile of customer
emails. You have neither. Most of that advice is not just premature here — it would be
actively worse than the deterministic workflow engine you already have.

Three things are worth building. Roughly eight things are worth deliberately **not**
building, and knowing why saves you more time than the features save.

| | Feature | Why it fits *this* system |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Payment proof reader (vision) | The only rich unstructured data you own is GCash screenshots — and one payment already has no typed reference at all, so the number exists *only* inside the image |
| **2** | Reply drafter | Generation needs no historical data. Works from lead #1 |
| **3** | Daily operations digest | Summarising structured data you already have; cheap and genuinely useful for a one-person team |

---

## 1. What the system actually looks like

### Data volume (production, at time of writing)

```
contacts        3      quotes      3      reviews     0
inquiries       7      payments    3      referrals   0
opportunities   7      activities  99     tasks      10
```

### What is already automated — without AI

`src/lib/workflows/definitions.ts` implements W1–W5 with **14 deterministic action types**:

```
upsert_contact  create_opportunity  send_message   notify_admin   create_task
add_tags        enroll              wait           exit_if        exit
move_stage      wait_until_trip_date  update_lifetime_value  issue_referral_rewards
```

That covers instant acknowledgement, speed-to-lead callback tasks, no-response drips,
quote chasing, booking confirmation, trip reminders, review requests and referral payouts.

**This is the single most important thing to understand before adding AI.** These rules are
free, instant, testable, and never hallucinate. Anywhere a rule can express the logic, a
rule should. AI earns its place only where rules genuinely cannot go: reading an image,
writing prose, or summarising a messy situation.

> ⚠️ **Pre-existing gap:** `vercel.json` has no `crons` key, so `src/app/api/cron/workflows/route.ts`
> is never invoked on a schedule. Every timed step in W2–W5 (`wait`, `wait_until_trip_date`)
> is therefore inert in production. Fix this before adding anything that depends on cron —
> including feature 3 below. It is worth more than any AI feature on this page.

## 2. The structural fact that shapes everything

**There is no natural-language text in your CRM.**

All 7 inquiries came through `contact-form` with an **empty `message` field**. The form
captures `serviceType`, `vehicleType`, `preferredDate`, `phone` as structured fields —
there is nothing to "understand". The three `message_in` activities are auto-generated by
the quote-accept flow, not real customer messages.

The actual conversation happens on **WhatsApp and Facebook Messenger**, and neither is
connected. WhatsApp is a click-to-chat `wa.me` deep link (`src/lib/messaging/index.ts`) —
there is no inbound webhook, so no customer message ever reaches the database.

Three consequences, and they set the whole strategy:

1. **Vision beats text.** The one place you hold rich unstructured data is payment
   screenshots stored as base64 in `payments.proof_data`. That is where an LLM has
   something real to do.
2. **Generation beats comprehension.** Drafting a reply needs no historical corpus.
   Classifying or scoring leads needs one you do not have.
3. **Getting the conversation into the CRM is a bigger lever than any AI feature.**
   Even a "log what the customer said" textarea on the lead screen would create the
   raw material that makes half of Tier 2 possible. Consider that before Tier 2.

## 3. Tier 1 — build these

### A. Payment proof reader (vision) ⭐ highest value

**The chore it removes.** Today an agent opens `/admin/payments/[id]`, squints at a GCash
screenshot, and cross-checks it against their banking app by eye before clicking Verify.
It is the highest-stakes repeated task in the portal and the slowest.

**Why it fits.** All three payments are GCash with a screenshot attached, and at least one
has `reference = null` — the customer never typed their receipt number, so the transaction
reference exists *only* inside the image. Extraction genuinely recovers data that is
otherwise unusable.

**How it works.**

1. On proof upload (or first admin view), send the image to a vision model.
2. Extract: `amount`, `referenceNumber`, `dateTime`, `senderName`, `recipientNumber`.
3. Cache the result on the row — a new `payments.ai_proof_read jsonb` column — so you pay
   once, not on every page load.
4. Render the extraction **beside the image**, with an automatic comparison against the
   quote:

```
Screenshot says      Expected            
₱4,000           ✓   ₱4,000   (quote total)
0917 804 6988    ✓   0917 804 6988  (your GCash)
Ref 0091234567   —   none given by customer
Aug 1, 6:25 AM   ✓   submitted 6:25 AM
```

**Guardrail — this is money, so it is advisory only.** It never sets `status`, never calls
`reviewPayment`, and never enables the Verify button on its own. A mismatch shows a warning;
a match shows a tick. A human still clicks Verify, and the existing verified-payment lock
still applies. The extraction sits next to the source image precisely so a hallucinated
number is obvious at a glance.

**Where it plugs in**

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `src/db/schema.ts` | add `aiProofRead: jsonb('ai_proof_read')` to `payments` |
| `src/lib/ai/read-proof.ts` | new — the vision call |
| `src/app/api/admin/payments/[id]/proof-read/route.ts` | new — `handleAdminRoute`, returns cached or computes |
| `src/app/admin/payments/[id]/page.tsx` | render the comparison panel |

**Effort:** ~1 day. **Model:** Haiku 4.5 — extraction is exactly what it is for.

---

### B. Reply drafter

**The chore it removes.** Speed-to-lead is described in your own W1 definition as *"the
biggest lever this business has."* The bottleneck is not knowing a lead arrived — W1 already
creates a 15-minute callback task — it is composing a good reply fast.

**Why it fits.** Generation requires no training data. It works on lead #1. And it handles
English / Taglish / Bisaya naturally, which a template library cannot.

**How it works.** A **Draft a reply** button on `/admin/inquiries/[id]` sends the model the
lead context you already have — service, vehicle, trip date, stage, quote status, activity
timeline, lead reference — plus a system prompt carrying your business voice, service list
and standing policies. It returns a WhatsApp-length message and an email-length variant.
The agent edits, then sends through the existing channels.

**Guardrail.** Draft-only. It populates a textarea; it never sends. When the agent does
send, log it via `logActivity` with `metadata: { aiDrafted: true }` so the timeline is
honest about what was machine-written. Never let it quote a price that is not already on a
quote record — put that in the system prompt and check it.

**Where it plugs in**

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `src/lib/ai/draft-reply.ts` | new |
| `src/app/api/admin/opportunities/[id]/draft-reply/route.ts` | new |
| `src/app/admin/inquiries/[id]/page.tsx` | button + editable draft box next to the existing note field |

**Effort:** ~1 day, most of it spent writing the voice prompt. Seed it with your best real
messages as few-shot examples — that is worth more than any prompt engineering trick.

**Model:** Sonnet 5. This is the one place output quality is customer-visible.

---

### C. Daily operations digest

**The chore it removes.** There is no single view answering "what needs me today?"
The dashboard shows metrics; it does not tell you what is slipping.

**How it works.** A daily cron reads the operational state — leads with no contact in 24h,
payments in `submitted`, trips within 48h with no driver assigned, overdue tasks, quotes
expiring — and asks the model to write a short WhatsApp-style briefing to the owner.

```
Good morning. 3 things today:
• L-001004 (Maria, van, Aug 14) — quote sent 3 days ago, no reply. Worth a call.
• ₱4,000 GCash from Joseph is waiting on verification since yesterday.
• Trip tomorrow (L-001001) still has no driver assigned.
```

**Why AI rather than a template.** A template can list the same rows. The model's value is
ordering by what actually matters today and staying quiet when nothing does — a static
digest that fires every morning with "0 items" gets muted within a week.

**Guardrail.** Read-only. It reports, it never acts. Send it to `ADMIN_NOTIFY_EMAIL` or as
a WhatsApp deep link.

**Depends on:** the cron gap above being fixed first.

**Effort:** ~half a day once cron works. **Model:** Haiku 4.5. **Cost:** ~1 call/day.

## 4. Tier 2 — later, when there is a reason

| Feature | Blocked on | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Quote line-item suggester** | Nothing — just lower value | Propose lines + prices in `QuoteBuilder` from a price list you supply. Useful once you are writing several quotes a day |
| **Review reply drafter + detractor triage** | `reviews` table is empty | The schema has `rating` and `nps`. Genuinely useful at ~20+ reviews. Pointless at 0 |
| **Inquiry free-text extraction** | No free text exists | Unblocks only if you add a message field customers actually use, or ingest WhatsApp. Do not build against a field that is empty in 7/7 rows |
| **Duplicate contact suggestions** | Volume | Intake already dedupes on normalised phone/email. AI would only catch "Jose" vs "Joseph" on different numbers — rare at 3 contacts |
| **Plain-language search** | Volume | "Who booked a van in August and hasn't paid?" is a nice demo. With 7 leads the existing filters are faster |

## 5. Tier 3 — do not build these

Being specific about the "no" list is the most useful part of this document.

**Lead scoring / win probability.** Needs hundreds of closed deals with outcomes. You have
7 opportunities. Worse, `pipeline_stages` already has a hand-set `probability` column that
drives the forecast — a number you choose will beat a model trained on 7 rows for years.

**Revenue forecasting.** Same problem, plus seasonality you have not lived through yet.

**Autonomous customer-facing chatbot.** Three reasons, any one sufficient: bookings involve
money, real vehicle availability the CRM does not track, and a driver who must actually turn
up. There is no inbound WhatsApp webhook, so the infrastructure does not exist. And a wrong
answer costs you a trip and a review.

**RAG / vector database.** Your entire CRM — every contact, lead, quote and activity —
comfortably fits in a single prompt with room to spare. Embeddings and a vector store would
be pure operational overhead solving a problem you will not have for several years. Just
pass the rows.

**Fine-tuning.** You do not have enough examples to fine-tune on, and prompting with a
handful of your own past messages as few-shot examples is both free and better.

**Auto-verifying payments.** Never. A hallucinated ₱4,000 releases a vehicle for a payment
that never arrived.

**Auto-sending AI-written messages.** Not until you have watched drafts for months and
trust them. The failure is public and lands with a customer.

**Replacing W1–W5 with an "AI agent".** The rules are deterministic, free, instant and
debuggable. Handing that to a model makes it slower, costlier, and non-reproducible.

## 6. Wiring it into this codebase

**Dependency.** There is currently no AI package. Add `@anthropic-ai/sdk`.

**Feature-flag it the way messaging already is.** `src/lib/messaging/index.ts` degrades
cleanly when `RESEND_API_KEY` is absent — logging instead of sending. Mirror that exactly:

```ts
const apiKey = process.env.ANTHROPIC_API_KEY;
if (!apiKey) return { available: false };   // UI hides the button; nothing breaks
```

This gives you a kill switch, keeps local dev free, and means a missing key is never an
outage.

**Never expose the key.** Server-side only — no `NEXT_PUBLIC_` prefix, ever.

**Follow the existing route pattern.** Every admin route uses `handleAdminRoute` from
`src/lib/auth/require-admin.ts`, with `runtime = 'nodejs'` and `dynamic = 'force-dynamic'`.
AI routes are no different. Set `maxDuration` — a vision call can exceed the default
function timeout.

**Cache every result on the row.** A `jsonb` column per feature. Re-reading the same
screenshot on every page load is the easiest way to turn a cheap feature into an expensive
one.

**Audit everything.** Log each AI-assisted action through `logActivity` with
`metadata: { ai: true, model, feature }`. You will want to know what was machine-generated
when something reads oddly six months from now.

**Model choice**

| Task | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Proof extraction, digest, classification | Haiku 4.5 | Fast, cheap, and extraction does not need a bigger model |
| Customer-facing drafts | Sonnet 5 | Output quality is visible to customers |
| — | Opus | No task here justifies it |

**Latency.** Your functions run in `sin1` (Singapore). API latency is fine. Show a spinner
on drafting; compute proof extraction on upload rather than on view so it is warm.

## 7. Guardrails (non-negotiable)

1. **AI never writes to money fields.** Not `payments.status`, not `quotes.total`,
   not `opportunities.monetaryValue`.
2. **AI never sends a message.** It fills a box a human sends from.
3. **Always show the source next to the output** — the screenshot beside its extraction.
4. **Mark AI-generated content on the timeline.** `metadata.aiDrafted`.
5. **Degrade to today's behaviour when the key is missing.** No key, no crash.
6. **Cap spend.** A per-day call ceiling and a `console.error` when it trips.
7. **Customer PII leaves your system.** Names, phone numbers and payment screenshots go to
   a third party. Anthropic does not train on API inputs by default — verify the current
   terms yourself before relying on it, and add a line to your privacy notice.

## 8. Cost

At your volume this is genuinely not a factor, which is itself an argument for building
Tier 1 rather than agonising over it.

| Feature | Tokens per call | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Proof read | ~2–3k in (image), ~200 out | once per payment |
| Reply draft | ~2–3k in, ~300 out | a few per lead |
| Daily digest | ~5k in, ~500 out | 1/day |

Even at **10× your current volume** — say 10 leads and 5 payments a day — this lands in the
low single-digit dollars per month. Check current per-token pricing before quoting a number
to anyone; the shape of the answer will not change.

## 10. How you will know it is working

Judge these on time saved, not on novelty.

- **Proof reader:** time from proof upload to Verify click. If it does not fall, the panel
  is not showing the right things.
- **Reply drafter:** how often a draft is sent with **no edits**. Below ~30%, the voice
  prompt needs your real messages in it, not more instructions.
- **Digest:** whether you still open it after three weeks. If you mute it, it is listing
  rather than prioritising.
- **All three:** count the corrections. A steady mismatch rate on proof extraction is a
  signal to narrow the prompt, not to trust it less selectively.

## Appendix — what changes the calculus

Revisit this document when any of these becomes true:

- **WhatsApp inbound is connected.** Unlocks conversation summarisation, intent detection
  and genuinely useful reply drafting. Biggest single unlock available to you.
- **~50 closed deals.** Lead scoring stops being nonsense — though a hand-tuned stage
  probability may still win.
- **~20 reviews.** Review drafting and detractor triage become worth the wiring.
- **More than one agent.** Routing, load balancing and consistency-of-voice start to matter.
- **A second service line or city.** Availability and pricing get complex enough that
  quote drafting earns real time back.
